Work

Intern Solutions Engineer (Co-op), Teldio

May – August 2026 · Ottawa

Teldio builds event-driven middleware that connects physical-security and operations systems to two-way radio and fleet software. The resume above lists what I shipped. This is the part that doesn't fit on one page.

Most of the term came down to one recurring constraint. An integration can only be trusted against the real system, and the field hardware on the other end of it — panels, radios, alarm equipment — doesn't virtualize. The server platforms I could stand up as VMs; the hardware I couldn't, so every test queued behind physical access to one bench. That queue is what Project Echo was built to remove: it records live protocol traffic off a module and replays it as test input, so a module can be exercised against a recording of the real thing instead of the thing itself. It outlived my term, which I care about more than any single integration I shipped.

The habit that actually mattered was refusing to trust one interface until a second one agreed with it. Bridging a vendor's .NET SDK into Go was only defensible because I could diff its events against the same vendor's REST API and show they were byte-identical. The defects worth finding were the same story from the other direction: they were invisible until two live things disagreed, and none of them would have surfaced against a mock.

My end-of-term review described the work as performed at the level of a junior solutions engineer rather than an intern. Details are kept general here because the specifics are Teldio's.
